## Context

In March 2026, two months into my full-time NAPP role, an editor I'd been working with on Search asked if I could help with something different. He was leading a summary workstream inside the Newsroom AI Initiative team — a separate group from NAPP, sitting inside the newsroom and focused on AI tools for reporters and editors. They wanted help thinking about how AI-generated summaries should appear to readers.

It started narrow: a single article, with an internal tool where editors could write prompts to produce summaries in different styles — bullet points, a paragraph, a longer recap. The first thing that resonated, in sketches, was a full-screen, Instagram-story-style summary card. From there I built out the framework — how this could work as a design system, what the summary primitives were, how editorial control surfaced in the UI.

The more I sat with it, the bigger it got. Article summary was one application. The same primitives could power a "catch me up" surface, a home-tab digest, a multi-article briefing, a multi-source synthesis — and not just in text. The expansion wasn't only mine — leadership conversations and work-shares I sat in on were already gesturing at the same shape. It resonated, and I spent the time sketching out what summary as a *capability* could look like across NYT.

## Approach

**The working thesis.** Most AI summaries on the market are text — natural language, quick, generated on demand. The working theory at NYT is that summary here should be different: not a replacement for the human report, but an *additive* layer. Engaging, often visual, often interactive, in support of the broader newsroom move toward multimodal storytelling. The point isn't to compress the story so a reader can skip it; the hope is to give them a useful entry, a satisfying angle, or a way back into reporting they wouldn't otherwise approach. None of this is settled — these are the differences we currently think are worth starting from.

<Margin kind="note">
  A market summary asks: how do I get the answer fast? The version we're sketching tries to ask: how do I make the journalism more reachable?
</Margin>

**Open questions.** What does "good" mean for an NYT summary, in a way that's testable? What does the editorial review workflow look like for AI-generated outputs in this workstream? How do different surfaces and brands need different voices? These are the questions the work is trying to answer — and they're still open. The system sketch below is one shape we're prototyping toward; it isn't the answer yet.

**System sketch (still exploratory).** The shape I've been sketching uses Scoop — NYT's internal publishing CMS — as the foundation. The idea: for any piece of journalism, an editor in Scoop can produce up to three base summary types — a paragraph, a bullet or multi-slide breakdown, and a richer one organized around evidence (quotes, numbers, charts, video). Each surface that wants to *show* a summary picks the base it needs and applies its own prompt at request time, shaping the output to the moment. An article-page "summarize this" tap, for example, would draw from the bullet base and produce a full-screen, Instagram-story-style card — because the reader's intent at that moment is *get the gist*, not leave the article behind.

<Margin kind="footnote" n={1}>
  This is still a sketch, not a shipped architecture. Leadership commitment is light; the shape is meant to invite review, not to declare a direction.
</Margin>
